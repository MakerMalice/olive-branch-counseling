Methods

Your goals lead. The tools follow.

I practice client-centered therapy and draw on different techniques to tailor treatment to you. Here are the main ones, in plain language.

Cognitive Behavioral Therapy (CBT)

CBT looks at the connection between thoughts, feelings and actions. Together we notice patterns that aren’t helping and practice new ways of responding. It’s often a good fit for anxiety, low mood, and stress, and for anyone who likes practical tools to use between sessions.

Play Therapy

Children often express through toys and games what they can’t yet say in words. Play therapy gives them a safe, familiar way to work through big feelings and experiences. It suits younger children especially.

Parent-Child Interaction Therapy (PCIT)

PCIT coaches parents in the moment while they play and interact with their child. The aim is a stronger, warmer bond and easier handling of challenging behavior. It’s a good fit for families with young children who want hands-on guidance.

Art Therapy

Drawing, painting and making things can open up feelings that are hard to talk about. No artistic skill is needed. It works well for children, teens and adults, particularly anyone who finds words difficult.

Motivational Interviewing

A collaborative, respectful conversation that helps you explore your own reasons for change, at your own pace. It’s especially helpful when you feel unsure or pulled in two directions, including around substance use and habits.
